01 · Learn

Putting symbols together.

A full transcription is just the symbols from the last four lessons, strung together with nothing extra added — no silent letters, no spelling quirks, just the sounds in order.

/bɔ̃ʒuʁ/

bonjour — hello

b + ɔ̃ (nasal) + ʒ + u + ʁ. Five sounds, five symbols, and the final r really is pronounced here — compare that to a silent final consonant you’ll see next.

/pəti/

petit — small

p + ə (schwa) + t + i. The final t is silent in this masculine form, so it’s simply absent from the transcription — nothing marks it, because there’s no sound there.

02 · Learn

Marking liaison and elision.

Two things worth flagging when you see them in a transcription:

‿

Liaison

A small tie mark (‿) between two words shows a liaison — a consonant that’s silent on its own but gets pronounced because the next word starts with a vowel sound. les amis is transcribed /le‿z‿ami/: the normally silent s surfaces as [z] because amis starts with a vowel.

’

Elision

When a word ending in a vowel (often e) is followed by a word starting with a vowel sound, the first vowel is often dropped entirely in speech — written with an apostrophe in normal French spelling (le + ami → l’ami), and simply left out of the transcription: /lami/.

03 · Learn

Reading practice: match the transcription.

A few more full transcriptions to read through before the quiz. Say each one aloud from the symbols alone, then press Listen to check yourself.

/mɛʁsi/

merci — thank you

m + ɛ + ʁ + s + i

/savabjɛ̃/

ça va bien — it’s going well

s + a + v + a + b + j (glide) + ɛ̃ (nasal). Notice the glide [j] gliding into the nasal vowel [ɛ̃] at the end — exactly the pattern from the semi-vowels lesson.

/œ̃bɔ̃vɛ̃blɑ̃/

un bon vin blanc — a good white wine

All four nasal vowels in one phrase, back from the nasal vowels lesson: œ̃, ɔ̃, ɛ̃, ɑ̃ — in that order.
