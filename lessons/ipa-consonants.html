IPA · Lesson 5

Consonants

Most French consonants are exactly what they look like. This lesson focuses on the handful — especially [ʁ], the French R — that aren’t.

Lesson Progress 0%

01 · Learn

Most consonants are exactly what you’d expect.

Good news first: most French consonants map directly onto familiar English sounds and symbols — p, b, t, d, k, ɡ, f, v, s, z, m, n, l all work almost exactly as you’d guess from their letters. A few spelling quirks are worth flagging, though:

s / z

A single s between vowels is [z]

maison is [mɛzɔ̃], not [mɛsɔ̃] — a single s surrounded by vowels is voiced. Double it (ss) to get the voiceless [s] back, as in poisson.

k

[k] hides behind several spellings

c before a/o/u, qu, and k itself all land on the same sound: cave, qui, and kilo all start with [k].

02 · Learn

The sounds that trip up English speakers.

These are the consonants worth real attention — either because English doesn’t have them, or because the instinct from English spelling leads you astray.

[ʁ]

rouge — red

The French R — made in the back of the throat, not with the tip of the tongue the way English R is. The single most distinctive sound in French pronunciation, and the subject of its own full lesson on this site.

[ʃ]

chat — cat

The “sh” sound, always spelled ch in French — never pronounced like English “ch” in “chip.”

[ʒ]

je — I

The voiced twin of [ʃ] — like the s in English “measure.” Spelled j, or g before e/i/y.

[ɲ]

montagne — mountain

A single consonant — tongue flat against the roof of the mouth — close to the ny in English “canyon,” always spelled gn.

03 · Learn

One more borrowed sound, and the full picture.

ŋ

camping — campsite

The “ng” sound in English “sing.” Not native to French, but common in words borrowed from English like parking and camping.

Put together with the familiar set from the first section, that’s the full French consonant inventory: p, b, t, d, k, ɡ, f, v, s, z, ʃ, ʒ, m, n, ɲ, ŋ, l, ʁ — eighteen sounds in total, the overwhelming majority of them familiar the moment you hear them.
