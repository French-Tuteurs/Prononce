IPA · Lesson 5

Consonants

All eighteen French consonant sounds, one dedicated card each — grouped by how they’re made so the patterns are easy to hear, with extra attention on [ʁ], the French R, and the handful of others English doesn’t have.

Lesson Progress 0%

01 · Learn

Eighteen sounds, three families.

French has eighteen consonant sounds. This lesson covers every one of them, grouped into three families: stops (p, b, t, d, k, ɡ — the sound cuts off completely before releasing), fricatives (f, v, s, z, ʃ, ʒ — air hisses through a narrow gap), and nasals, liquids, and the French R (m, n, ɲ, ŋ, l, ʁ). Most of these are exactly what you’d expect from their letters — the goal here is to flag the handful that aren’t.

02 · Learn

Stops: a full cutoff, then a release.

These six sounds close off airflow completely for an instant before releasing. French stops are unaspirated — no small puff of air on release, unlike English p, t, and k at the start of a word. Hold your hand in front of your mouth while saying “pain” in English, then in French — the English one puffs, the French one doesn’t.

[p]

pain — bread

Voiceless, lips together, no puff of air on release — crisper and softer than English p.

[b]

beau — beautiful

The voiced twin of [p] — lips together, vocal cords buzzing throughout. Essentially identical to English b.

[t]

tasse — cup

Voiceless, but dental — tongue touches the back of the upper teeth, not the ridge behind them the way English t does. No puff of air.

[d]

dos — back

The voiced twin of [t] — same dental tongue position, vocal cords buzzing. Close to English d.

[k]

cave — cellar

Voiceless, back of the tongue against the soft palate. Hides behind several spellings: c before a/o/u, qu, and k itself all land here — cave, qui, and kilo all start with [k].

[ɡ]

gare — station

The voiced twin of [k]. Spelled g before a/o/u, or gu before e/i/y to keep the hard sound — compare gare to guide.

03 · Learn

Fricatives: air hissing through a gap.

These six come in three voiceless/voiced pairs — same mouth position, the only difference is whether the vocal cords are buzzing. Two of them, [ʃ] and [ʒ], don’t exist as single letters in English spelling but are everywhere in French.

[f]

fête — party

Voiceless, top teeth on bottom lip — identical to English f. Spelled f or ph.

[v]

vous — you

The voiced twin of [f] — identical to English v.

[s]

sac — bag

Voiceless, like English s. Spelled s at the start of a word or next to a consonant, ss between vowels, or c/ç before e/i/y.

[z]

zéro — zero

The voiced twin of [s]. The spelling trap: a single s between vowels is also [z] — maison is [mɛzɔ̃], not [mɛsɔ̃]. Double it (ss) to get [s] back, as in poisson.

[ʃ]

chat — cat

The “sh” sound, always spelled ch in French — never pronounced like English “ch” in “chip.”

[ʒ]

je — I

The voiced twin of [ʃ] — like the s in English “measure.” Spelled j, or g before e/i/y.

04 · Learn

Nasals, liquids, and the French R.

The last six: two nasal consonants you already know from English, one you don’t, a light French l, a borrowed English sound, and the French R — the single most distinctive sound in the language.

[m]

merci — thank you

Lips together, air through the nose — identical to English m.

[n]

nez — nose

Tongue tip against the teeth, air through the nose — identical to English n.

[ɲ]

montagne — mountain

A single consonant — tongue flat against the roof of the mouth — close to the ny in English “canyon,” always spelled gn.

[ŋ]

camping — campsite

The “ng” sound in English “sing.” Not native to French, but common in words borrowed from English like parking and camping.

[l]

lune — moon

Lighter and more forward than English l — tongue tip against the teeth the whole time, none of the “dark” back-of-mouth quality English l can have at the end of a word.

[ʁ]

rouge — red

The French R — made in the back of the throat, not with the tip of the tongue the way English R is. The single most distinctive sound in French pronunciation, and the subject of its own full lesson on this site.

That’s all eighteen: p, b, t, d, k, ɡ, f, v, s, z, ʃ, ʒ, m, n, ɲ, ŋ, l, ʁ — the complete French consonant inventory.
