Semi-Vowels (Glides)

Three quick, vowel-like sounds that behave like consonants — including one, [ɥ], that has no English equivalent at all.

Lesson Progress 0%

01 · Learn

A sound caught between a vowel and a consonant.

A semi-vowel (also called a glide) starts in a vowel-like tongue position but moves so quickly that it behaves like a consonant — it never gets to “settle” into a full vowel. English has two of these too: the y in “yes” and the w in “west.” French has those same two, plus a third with no English equivalent at all.

Semi-vowels always appear right before another vowel in the same syllable — that quick glide into the following vowel is exactly what makes them semi-vowels rather than full ones.

02 · Learn

The three symbols.

[j]

yeux — eyes

Identical to English y in “yes.” Also appears spelled -ill- and -il, as in fille and travail.

[w]

oui — yes

Identical to English w in “west.” Usually spelled oi or ou before a vowel.

[ɥ]

huit — eight

The one with no English equivalent: round your lips as if for [y] (the vowel in tu), then glide immediately into the next vowel. Almost always spelled u before another vowel.

03 · Learn

Hearing the difference: full vowel vs. glide.

The same letters can be a full vowel in one word and a glide in another — it depends entirely on whether another vowel follows in the same syllable.

i / j

si vs. sien

if vs. his/hers — [si] (full vowel [i]) vs. [sjɛ̃] (glide [j] into the nasal vowel [ɛ̃])

u / ɥ

tu vs. tuer

you vs. to kill — [ty] (full vowel [y]) vs. [tɥe] (glide [ɥ] into [e])
